## Project name

SolSurvivor

## High-level summary (about 100 words)

SolSurvivor is a browser game for kids aged 7 to 12 who run a small outpost on Mars
or at the Moon's south pole. Every sol they share out too little power between air,
food, a storm shelter, science and the radio to Earth, and see their choices play out
at a 3D base. Every storm, site and photo comes from a real NASA mission, and live
data shows this week's solar flares and today's radio delay to Mars. Teachers get
class mission codes, a teacher's key and printable logs; kids can play in teams of up
to four on one computer, in English or Arabic, even offline.

## What it does

- Teaches trade-offs and planning ahead: plants take 2 sols, storm warnings come a sol early,
  and only if the radio is on. A live preview shows where every meter will be tonight.
- Teaches space weather: why solar storms are dangerous where there is little air and no
  global magnetic field, and how shielding helps.
- Rewards habits, not luck: badges for listening to Earth and using the shelter on storm nights.
- Works in classrooms: a code gives the whole class the same storms on the same sols, so
  students can compare choices; the debrief turns what happened into discussion questions.

## NASA data and resources used

| Resource | How the game uses it |
|---|---|
| NASA DONKI (api.nasa.gov) | This week's solar flares on the title screen (NOAA SWPC GOES feed as a fallback when DONKI is down) |
| JPL "Approximate Positions of the Planets" | Today's Earth–Mars radio delay, computed in the browser and tested against the 2003 and 2018 close approaches |
| Curiosity RAD, MAVEN, Mars Odyssey MARIE, LRO CRaTER | The solar storms of 2024, 2017, 2003 and 1972 and why shelter matters |
| MOXIE, Veggie (ISS), plants in Apollo soil | The oxygen maker and greenhouse |
| Opportunity, InSight, Spirit | Dust storms, dusty panels and wind cleanings |
| Perseverance, Ingenuity, Phoenix | Expeditions in Jezero Crater, helicopter flights, the ice drill |
| LRO, LCROSS, Apollo seismometers, Artemis II | Moon sites, ice, moonquakes, long shadows and the Aitken basin photo |
| NASA Image and Video Library | 15 photos, each with its NASA ID |
| NASA Mars Trek (Viking MDIM 2.1), NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit | The globes in the opening shot |

## Tools

React, TypeScript, Vite, React Three Fiber (three.js), zustand, Vitest, Playwright, axe-core.

## Use of AI (disclosure)

The team used Claude Code (Anthropic) as a coding assistant to write and test the code,
the Arabic draft text and the demo video recorder. The team chose the design, checked the
NASA facts and sources, and reviewed and playtested the result. The Arabic text still needs
a native speaker's review. No AI-generated images, voices or video are in the game: all
photos and globes are NASA's, and the music is generated by code at play time.

## Project history

The team prototyped the idea before the event. This repository was written from scratch
during the hackathon weekend; its commit history shows when each part was built.

## Privacy and safety

No sign-in, no names, no tracking, no ads. Progress stays in the browser. Live data
requests carry no personal information. Playtests follow docs/PLAYTEST.md: no photos,
video or voice of children.
